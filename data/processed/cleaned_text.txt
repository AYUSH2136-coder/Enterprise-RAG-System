ABSTRACT
While Large Language Models (LLMs) exhibit unprecedented cognitive reasoning within ster-
ile, curated benchmarks, they remain fundamentally brittle when confronted with the stochastic
reality of real-world human-computer interaction. In production environments, user inputs are
rarely pristine; they are frequently degraded by typographical errors, syntactic inconsistencies,
informal vernacular, and semantic distractors. Conventional fine-tuning paradigms, which rely
on idealized datasets, fail to bridge this robustness gap, leading to catastrophic reasoning
failures when models encounter high-entropy inputs.
This project introduces a novel framework for fortifying LLMs against such vulnerabilities:
Curriculum-Based Noise Injection for Synthetic Data Generation. The core innovation lies
in a graduated adversarial engine that systematically transforms golden instructional data into
high-entropy synthetic variants. By algorithmically modulating noise levelsspanning struc-
tural degradation to semantic interferencethis methodology forces the model to prioritize
latent intent over surface-level syntax.
Leveraging a technology stack comprising Python, nlpaug, and the Hugging Face PEFT
ecosystem, we implement Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) on open-weight foundation models.
Our methodology demonstrates that by exposing models to a curriculum of increasing linguistic
complexity, they develop a robust selective attention mechanism. Rather than memorizing rigid
patterns, the robustified models learn to actively filter out environmental noise, resulting
in resilient reasoning pathways that maintain high performance across diverse, non-idealized
prompts. This research establishes a scalable, resource-efficient pipeline for training the next
generation of deployment-ready, noise-invariant artificial intelligence.
Keywords: Large Language Models, Synthetic Data Generation, Curriculum Learning, Model
Robustness, Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT), LoRA, Adversarial Training.

CHAPTER 1
Introduction
1.1
Core Problem: The Assumption of Pristine Context
Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated exceptional capabilities in automated code
generation, data analysis, and logical reasoning. However, current benchmark evaluations of-
ten rely on a critical underlying assumption: the expectation of perfectly formatted, highly
structured, and strictly relevant input prompts.
In practical software engineering and data science deployments, input prompts frequently con-
tain high levels of linguistic and structural entropy. Users may introduce typographical errors,
omit standard formatting, utilize colloquial syntax, or inadvertently include contextually ir-
relevant information. When subjected to this out-of-distribution entropy, foundational LLMs
frequently experience a phenomenon described as catastrophic distractibility. The models
attention mechanism becomes saturated by extraneous tokens, leading to syntactic degradation,
incorrect API utilization, or a failure to execute the primary logical instruction.
1.2
Motivation: Architectural Robustification
Current industry practices attempt to mitigate this distractibility primarily through heuristic
prompt engineering, such as appending rigid system instructions directing the model to filter
noise. While functional in constrained environments, this approach does not address the under-
lying architectural vulnerability. For LLMs to achieve reliable autonomy, they require inherent
cognitive resilience. The architecture must possess the mathematical capability to process high-
entropy inputs, autonomously attenuate attention weights on irrelevant tokens, and successfully
map the core logical instruction to the correct output distribution.
1.3
The Proposed Solution: Curriculum Noise Injection
To address this vulnerability, this research proposes a systemic robustification framework.
Rather than modifying the input heuristics, this project alters the models fundamental weight
distributions. By engineering a Curriculum Noise Engine, this project generates a synthetic
dataset that intentionally introduces graduated levels of linguistic entropy into pristine cod-

CHAPTER 2
Literature Review
2.1
The Baseline: Achieving Zero-Shot Logic on Clean Data
The foundation of modern LLM reasoning was established by optimizing models on pristine
instructional data. Wang et al. [1] demonstrated with the Self-Instruct framework that training
models on high-quality, self-generated instructions drastically improves their ability to follow
complex logical steps. Core Contribution: They successfully established that LLMs can func-
tion as powerful reasoning engines when aligned with structured data. Methodological Lim-
itation: Because this paradigm optimizes exclusively for crystallized knowledge on clean
datasets, the resulting models remain highly vulnerable to the out-of-distribution, high-entropy
inputs characteristic of real-world deployments.
2.2
Discovering the Flaw: The Distractibility Phenomenon
The fragility of these models under sub-optimal conditions was formally diagnosed by Shi et
al. [2]. Utilizing the Grade-School Math with Irrelevant Context (GSM-IC) benchmark, they
investigated model accuracy when irrelevant semantic information was injected into reason-
ing prompts. Core Contribution: They empirically proved the existence of catastrophic dis-
tractibility, documenting significant performance degradation across major foundation models.
Methodological Limitation: While foundational for diagnostic purposes, their proposed mit-
igations relied primarily on prompt-engineering rather than structural weight updates, leaving
the fundamental architectural vulnerability unresolved.
2.3
Early Remediation Attempts: Random Data Augmenta-
tion
To harden models against noise, researchers have historically relied on data augmentation. Wei
and Zou [3] introduced EDA (Easy Data Augmentation), a technique that injects character-level
noise (synonym swaps, random deletions) into training sets. What they achieved: They proved
that adding slight noise prevents overfitting and boosts text classification accuracy. Where
they fell short: EDA treats noise as a randomized, binary state. When arbitrary, un-curated

noise is injected into highly complex reasoning or strict-syntax tasks (like SQL or Pandas code
generation), it overwhelms the models attention mechanism, leading to training collapse rather
than resilience.
2.4
The Training Paradigm: Curriculum Learning
To prevent the training collapse caused by random noise, Bengio et al. [4] proposed Curriculum
Learning, a strategy inspired by human cognitive development. What they achieved: They
proved that models converge significantly faster, and reach better local minima, when training
data is introduced in gradually increasing tiers of difficulty, rather than randomized batches.
2.5
The Architecture Fix: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning
Re-training foundation models to adapt to new datasets is traditionally constrained by mas-
sive computational costs. Hu et al. [5] revolutionized this with LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation).
What they achieved: They proved that injecting small, trainable rank decomposition matrices
into the Transformers attention layers (q proj and v proj) allows a model to learn entirely
new behaviors while freezing the base weights, utilizing only a fraction of the standard GPU
memory.
2.6
Synthesis and the Research Gap
While the aforementioned literature provides a strong historical foundation, synthesizing these
studies reveals the critical research gap this project addresses.
The industry currently understands that models are easily distracted (Shi et al.), and it possesses
the tools to alter model weights efficiently (Hu et al.) and structure difficult training phases
(Bengio et al.). However, no existing framework successfully combines these elements to
actively cure the distractibility flaw in strict-syntax programming domains. This project
fills that exact gap by utilizing Curriculum Learning to inject adversarial noise, and employing
LoRA to fundamentally harden the models selective attention against real-world entropy.

CHAPTER 3
Problem Formulation and Objectives
3.1
Problem Statement
Despite the rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) in code generation and
logical reasoning, foundational architectures exhibit a critical vulnerability: catastrophic dis-
tractibility. When presented with out-of-distribution, high-entropy prompts containing irrel-
evant semantic context, typos, or structural degradation, the Self-Attention mechanism of the
LLM fails to accurately isolate the primary instruction (the golden intent).
In strict-syntax domains such as Pandas dataframe manipulation, SQL querying, and Bash
scripting, this failure in selective attention leads to hallucinated code, incorrect library utiliza-
tion, or complete reasoning collapse. The core problem this project addresses is the lack of
inherent cognitive resilience in foundational LLMs against adversarial or noisy user prompts.
3.2
Proposed Solution
To mitigate this vulnerability, this project proposes a framework for actively hardening the
reasoning pathways of an LLM through Curriculum-Based Noise Injection and Parameter-
Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) [4, 5].
Rather than relying on superficial prompt-engineering workarounds, the proposed solution fun-
damentally updates the models weights. By algorithmically generating a synthetic dataset that
gradually introduces increasing levels of linguistic entropy (ranging from character-level typos
to full semantic distractors) [3, 2], and subsequently fine-tuning the model using Low-Rank
Adaptation (LoRA) [5], the model is forced to learn robust feature extraction. The objective is
to train the attention heads to assign near-zero weight to irrelevant semantic distractors while
preserving the functional logic required to generate syntactically perfect code.
3.3
Detailed Technical Objectives
To successfully implement the proposed solution, the project is divided into four distinct tech-
nical objectives:

1. Development of a Curriculum Noise Engine: To design and implement a Python-based

4. Empirical Evaluation and Benchmarking: To evaluate the cognitive resilience of the
robustified model against the base model using a Regex-based Token Overlap methodol-
ogy, proving a statistical reduction in the models distractibility.
3.4
Scope and Limitations
While this project establishes a scalable pipeline for robustification, its scope is bound by the
following constraints:
 Model Size Limitation: Due to hardware memory constraints (VRAM availability), the
fine-tuning phase is strictly limited to the 3-Billion parameter class of models (specifi-
cally Qwen 2.5 Coder 3B).
 LoRA Rank Capacity: The low-rank dimension is constrained to r = 16. While highly
efficient, this restricts the volume of new parameters the model can learn, which may
cause gradient interference in highly complex, multi-step logic generation (such as ad-
vanced nested SQL joins or complex Pandas aggregations).

4.3
Curriculum Noise Engine Implementation
To achieve this attention-weight adjustment without causing training collapse, the dataset was
generated using Curriculum Learning principles [4]. The Python-based Noise Engine algo-
rithms incrementally increase cognitive entropy across four levels.
4.3.1
Level 1 and 2: Character and Structural Entropy
Level 1 injects character-level perturbations (e.g., typos, swapped letters) utilizing randomized
heuristic functions. If a word w consists of characters [c1, c2, . . . , cn], the engine applies a swap
permutation with a probability p = 0.15. Level 2 destroys the structural integrity of the prompt
by algorithmically stripping markdown formatting, code block delimiters, and standard casing,
forcing the model to rely purely on syntax rather than visual structure.
4.3.2
Level 3: Syntactic Entropy (Slang Injection)
Level 3 introduces out-of-distribution vernacular. Standard programming prompts are mathe-
matically mapped to a dictionary of colloquialisms (e.g., translating create a scatter plot to
yo cn u plzz drw a scttr plot). This forces the LLMs tokenizer to map rare, non-academic
tokens to standard execution pathways.
4.3.3
Level 4: Semantic Distraction
Inspired by the GSM-IC benchmark [2], Level 4 explicitly attacks the models selective at-
tention. The algorithm calculates the token length of the prompt, identifies the midpoint, and
strategically inserts a completely irrelevant factual string (e.g., The capital of France is Paris)
directly into the core instruction.

Implementation
5.1
Dataset Generation and Preprocessing
The implementation of the robustification pipeline commenced with the curation of a pristine
instruction dataset. The baseline data was sourced from standard coding benchmark subsets,
comprising 5,000 syntactically perfect prompt-response pairs across Pandas, SQL, and Bash
domains.
5.2
Curriculum Noise Injection Engine
The algorithmic degradation was implemented via a custom Python module (noise engine.py).
The script utilizes the nlpaug library alongside custom heuristic functions to inject entropy.
 L1 & L2 Implementation: Character permutations and structural stripping were exe-
cuted using regex-based text parsing, applying a randomized 0.15 probability mask over
the prompt tokens.
 L4 Distractor Implementation: A secondary Python script calculated the token length
of the input sequence, mathematically isolated the midpoint, and injected a string ran-
domly sampled from a curated dictionary of 500 semantically irrelevant factual state-
ments.

3B foundation model was instantiated using the HuggingFace transformers library. To
circumvent VRAM Out-of-Memory (OOM) failures, the base weights were quantized to 4-bit
NormalFloat (NF4) using the bitsandbytes library. The LoRA adapter was configured via
the peft framework with r = 16,  = 32, and a dropout rate of 0.05, targeting exclusively
the q proj and v proj attention matrices. Training was orchestrated using the standard
autoregressive Cross-Entropy loss over 3 epochs.

Limitations and Challenges
6.6.1
Domain-Specific Complexity Bottlenecks
While the curriculum training was highly successful for structural languages (Bash, HTML,
SQL), the model struggled to achieve the same resilience in the Pandas domain. The base model
achieved 0.60 accuracy on Level 4 Pandas distractors, while the robustified model scored 0.40.
This limitation suggests that when a domain requires highly complex, multi-step programmatic

reasoning (such as chaining Pandas DataFrame manipulations), the r = 16 LoRA adapter
may reach a parameter capacity bottleneck. The limited trainable parameter space was forced
to choose between learning the complex syntax and filtering the noise, resulting in gradient
interference.
6.6.2
Data Generation Constraints
A primary challenge identified during the deployment of the Curriculum Noise Engine was
the inherent limitation of its heuristic-based injection mechanisms. While methods such as
random-index distractor injections and character-level substitutions are effective for simulating
environmental entropy, they operate without an innate understanding of the underlying instruc-
tional logic.
The specific constraints encountered include:
Semantic Integrity Erosion: Because the engine targets tokens stochastically, it occasionally
corrupted pivotal tokenssuch as logical operators (e.g., not, if, then), key entities, or
specific constraints within the prompt. This transformed the data from noisy yet interpretable
into a state of logical unsolvability, where the original intent was entirely lost.
The Over-Corruption Threshold: There exists a thin boundary between adversarial noise
and total information loss. In certain high-entropy curriculum stages, the heuristic approach
unintentionally triggered catastrophic interference, generating synthetic samples where the
ground-truth labels no longer logically followed the corrupted input.
Contextual Blindness: Since the heuristics rely on positional indices rather than semantic im-
portance, the system could not distinguish between a distractor word and a functional word.
Consequently, the resulting prompts sometimes lacked the necessary coherence for the model
to perform successful selective attention, leading to a training signal that was counterproductive
to the goal of robust reasoning.
This constraint highlights a critical trade-off in synthetic data generation: as the complexity of
the noise increases, the risk of semantic drift necessitates more sophisticated validation or a
human-in-the-loop filtering process to ensure the training data remains viable.

CHAPTER 7
Conclusion and Future Scope
7.1
Conclusion
The primary objective of this research was to diagnose and actively mitigate the catastrophic
distractibility inherent in modern Large Language Models when processing strict-syntax do-
mains. This objective was successfully achieved through the design and implementation of a
novel Curriculum-Based Noise Injection framework.
By systematically exposing the Qwen 2.5 3B foundation model to a 4-tier graduation of cogni-
tive entropyranging from character-level typos to Level 4 semantic distractorsthe models
reasoning pathways were successfully hardened.
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)
proved highly capable of updating the Transformers attention matrices to filter out irrelevant
context without triggering catastrophic forgetting of core coding syntax.
The empirical evaluations yielded highly significant results. In highly fragile domains such as
Bash scripting, the base model suffered total reasoning collapse (0.0 accuracy) under semantic
noise. Post-fine-tuning, the robustified model achieved a 1.0 accuracy score against Level 3
perturbations and a 0.60 score against Level 4 semantic distractors. Furthermore, the robus-
tification process unexpectedly improved the models baseline zero-shot logic, increasing its
accuracy on perfectly clean SQL and HTML prompts. Ultimately, this project proves that al-
gorithmic noise injection, when structured as a pedagogical curriculum, fundamentally alters
and improves an LLMs selective attention mechanisms.
7.2
Future Scope
While this project establishes a robust framework for structural and semantic resilience, it opens
several promising avenues for future expansion and advanced graduate-level Artificial Intelli-
gence research:
 Development of Persona-Resilient Architectures: The mechanisms proven in this studytraining
an attention layer to ignore adversarial distractorscan be inverted to create highly re-
silient, context-locked persona agents. Future work will explore developing specialized
behavioral architectures (such as a highly specific personality clown model) that can

maintain a strict, unbreakable character profile even when subjected to intense adversarial
prompt injections or user-driven context switching.
 Scaling to Multimodal Attention Vectors: The current curriculum engine is restricted
to textual entropy. Future expansions of this research could apply the same graduated
curriculum methodology to Vision-Language Models (VLMs), training the architecture
to ignore visual distractors (e.g., irrelevant background objects or visual noise) when
interpreting technical diagrams or code screenshots.
 Dynamic Curriculum Adjustment via Reinforcement Learning: The current noise

extension would integrate Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or
AI-feedback (RLAIF) to dynamically adjust the difficulty of the injected noise during the

tuning on larger distributed clusters can resolve gradient interference in highly complex
reasoning domains.
